# CUDA & OptiX optimisation checklist — Pass 2

**Repository:** [gregorycarnegie/black_hole](https://github.com/gregorycarnegie/black_hole) · **Audit date:** 24 November 2026 · **Revision:** [`e9ce1b95f31602d84503066ecc552884528e2a01`](https://github.com/gregorycarnegie/black_hole/commit/e9ce1b95f31602d84503066ecc552884528e2a01) · **Target:** RTX 4090 / Ada SM 8.9; retain SM 8.0 compatibility.

## Executive decision

Prioritise cheaper and more accurate geodesic integration, reducing live state, and long-tail scheduling. Keep the software space-time BVH as default. Do not migrate the entire geodesic loop into OptiX. The best possible single-hole architectural improvement is a specialised separable Kerr solver with analytic or semi-analytic vacuum propagation and numerical transfer where required; it cannot replace the binary or Ellis solver. All proposed performance improvements below are hypotheses, not measured gains. The first-pass report already covered baseline profiling, early capture, interop, register pressure, GAS refits, SBT reuse, payloads and stack sizing. This document extends those items, rather than claiming they are new.

## Prioritised work

Impact/effort/risk are qualitative engineering estimates. P0 = prerequisites and first experiments; P1 = larger algorithmic changes; P2 = optional or conditional. Suggested speed gates are engineering policy, not predictions: retain a change only if median trace time improves at least 10% on a relevant workload (or improves validated accuracy at comparable cost), p95 does not materially regress, and accuracy gates pass. A lower threshold may be justified for low-risk maintenance. Compare both experimental flags off and on, and record individual effects.

- [ ] **P0.1 — Reproduce profiling at the pinned revision.** Impact high / effort low / risk low. Use fixed resolution, camera, spin, sample/jitter sequence and disk preset. Record driver, compiler/LLVM revision, PTX hash, block shape, clocks, temperature and GPU memory; run at least 30 timed samples after warm-up in alternating A/B order, repeat across sessions. Use source correlation only where cuda-oxide emits usable line info. Profile without diagnostic instrumentation for timing; instrument separate passes for counts. Keep the EHT snapshot separate from live rendering. [R1, R2, R3]
- [ ] **P0.2 — Finish an independent reference and define correctness gates before performance changes.** Impact high / effort medium / risk low. The existing Zig reference is not converged at the hardest settings. Improve it with higher-order integration, analytic MKS derivatives and FP64/extended precision where needed; require a convergence study and cross-check exact isolated Kerr with an independent separated solution. Use E, Lz, Q, null Hamiltonian, screen transport, redshift and event classification; independently compare vacuum direction. Capture and escape only when validated; unresolved is an explicit outcome, not a black pixel claimed as capture. Existing targets: position and momentum 1e-6 times max(1, infinity norm), escape direction 1e-6 rad, screen/redshift errors 1e-6, Stokes absolute 1e-6 of fixed reference intensity and relative intensity 1e-4 above floor. Report misses rather than weakening targets. [R4–R6, R10]
- [x] **P0.3 — Promote guarded early capture after wider validation; re-profile the remaining workload.** **Done 2026-11-25:** early capture is the default in every render kernel; `--full-trace` (bit 0, now inverted) turns it off, and the in-kernel guard is unchanged. Guard review: plasma is zero at projected disk radius ≤ r_in for every disk model, tilt and warp, the projected radius never exceeds the Kerr radius, and swarm orbits (≥ 12 r_g) lie outside the largest ISCO (9 r_g). `early_capture_matches_the_full_trace` requires bit-identical images at 8 spins (0 … ±0.995, 0.999), 4 disk models, 3 elevations, 3 polarization views and the binary, and confirms 336 early-captured pixels captured in binary64 on the CPU; `swarm_early_capture_matches_the_full_trace` does the same for brute force, BVH and OptiX at 256/1,024/4,096 bodies; the equatorial-potential test adds ±0.995. Close-up re-profile: 91.3 → 19.5 ms still, 91.7 → 17.6 ms orbit; accepted steps 42.80M → 15.04M; slowest 5% now 7.8% of steps (was 56.9%), and disk-limited steps (6.39M) are the main remaining workload. Finite precision: a ray the binary64 guard accepts must also pass an outward-rounded interval certificate (`kerr_capture_certified`, inward tangent from the state's momentum), so each decision holds for the exact stored state. The CPU twin `certified::capture_certified` confirms all 20,129 GPU captures of random states at 7 spins and all 3,241 equatorial ones. The cost is about 2% of frame time, with the guard kept out of line. Still outside proof: integration error of the stored state, and a Lean proof of the KS-Cartesian Carter identity. Impact high / effort medium / risk medium. Existing experiment is already strong; retain guard for single unit-mass origin-centred Kerr, aligned disk, black surface, |a|≤0.995 and vacuum support, plus fallback. Do not enable for binary, shifted/rotated configurations, swarm intersections or unsupported plasma without proof of applicability; include all enabled geometry/transfer semantics in guard review. Expand boundary tests and prove finite precision where possible. Recompute slow-tail statistics after enabling the experiment: pre-optimisation 56.8% share is not the current distribution. [R4]
- [x] **P0.4 — Test Richardson extrapolation as an accuracy-first low-cost control.** **Done 2026-11-24: rejected and reverted.** It made the near-critical trajectories worse (impact 5.25: 4.1e-3 versus 1.6e-3 position error). Changing `STEP_TOLERANCE` tenfold looser left step counts and errors unchanged: the geometric limit sets the step. (The follow-up P1.1 prototype showed that the step-doubling estimate was rounding-blind, not that the error is purely roundoff.) Details are in [accuracy audit](verification/accuracy-performance.md). Original plan: Impact medium / effort medium / risk medium. `advance_ray_counting` already computes coarse and fine RK4 states; test `fine + (fine-coarse)/15` componentwise where the asymptotic local-error regime is valid. Compare at equal error, not just equal nominal tolerance; recompute or validate extrapolated endpoint metric, null constraint, screen gauge and event ordering; disable across nonsmooth/discontinuous regions. Existing accuracy audit already suggests this experiment. Do not reuse endpoint derivative for a different state. If no improvement in error/cost, revert. [R7]

### P1 — Reduce the cost of the CUDA integrator

- [x] **P1.1 — Prototype an embedded Dormand–Prince RK5(4) controller (FSAL).** **Prototype done 2026-11-24:** `--experiment-dp45` (opt-in, no FSAL). It makes single-hole and binary frames 30–34% faster. Near-critical trajectory errors fall about threefold, and EHT snapshot accuracy is unchanged (I L2 0.704% versus 0.719%). See [RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md](RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md), section "Experimental Dormand–Prince controller". Follow-up work is in P1.1b. Original plan: Impact high / effort medium-high / risk high. In [`tools/cuda/kernels.rs:1385–1603`](https://github.com/gregorycarnegie/black_hole/blob/e9ce1b95f31602d84503066ecc552884528e2a01/tools/cuda/kernels.rs#L1385-L1603), preserve the RK4 controller and add an opt-in alternative using one embedded stage sequence. Seven RHS stages, potentially six new evaluations on the next smooth accepted step if its initial derivative exactly equals the previous final derivative. This is a stage-count hypothesis, not a time estimate; current RK4 reuses work and also has endpoint checks. For comparison, the current step-doubling controller (`advance_ray_counting`) costs 11 RHS and 11 `scene_metric` evaluations per first-try accepted step: initial derivative, 3 coarse, 3 first-half, 1 midpoint derivative, 3 second-half, plus the endpoint metric. The P0.4 run showed the geometric limit, not the error tolerance, setting the step on near-critical rays. So expect any gain from fewer evaluations per step at a similar step count, not from longer steps. Accumulate weighted stage sums rather than keeping all matrices live. Enforce stage validity, null constraint, screen gauge, disk/geometry bounds, retry budget and event ordering; invalidate FSAL when state changes, including projection/gauge adjustments. Compare error-controlled step counts, RHS/metric evaluations, register/local memory and timing; do not assume higher order helps when the disk or geometry determines step size. Benchmark binary too, where separability is unavailable. [R7, R8]
- [x] **P1.1b — Make the Dormand–Prince controller the default.** **Done 2026-11-25:** DP45 is the default in every render kernel (`bh_geodesic`, early capture, swarm, OptiX, diagnostics); `--rk4-controller` (experiment bit 2, now inverted) keeps step-doubling RK4 for one release. The windowed close-up takes 92 ms per frame against 194 ms with `--rk4-controller`. **Status 2026-11-24:** (1)–(4) done; (5) waits on P1.6. (1) The swarm is 34–44% faster with DP45 on the BVH and 24–40% faster on OptiX, and both still match brute force bit for bit. (2) Both outliers top the list of controller disagreements, and the binary64 Kerr reference favours DP45 (9.1e-2 → 1.6e-2 and 2.1e-2 → 4.1e-3 rad). (3) A DP45-only `bh_geodesic` needs 255 registers and 320 local bytes, against 704 for RK4, and halves the frame time. Its images are identical to the experiments kernel's, so the stage sums need no restructuring. (4) `verification/BlackHole/DormandPrince.lean` proves the tableau's order conditions (exactly five; embedded exactly four), FSAL and the error weights. `tests/dormand_prince_tableau.rs` checks that the kernel's `DP_A`/`DP_B`/`DP_E` literals are those rationals. The controller's acceptance rules are not certified (N3). (5) **Decision 2026-11-24: DP45 stays opt-in; P1.6's unresolved band gates the switch.** DP45 gives 2 wrong near-critical classifications per 256-ray run where RK4 gives 0. Across step factors 0.11/0.12/0.13, though, RK4 gives 5/0/9 wrong and DP45 1/2/2 (14 against 5). The sweep stays a diagnostic, and the zero-wrong requirement for definitive capture/escape answers stays. Flip the default once both controllers pass it under the P1.6 band. **2026-11-24: both now pass under the band**, so the default switch is unblocked. Impact high / effort low-medium / risk medium. Before switching: (1) time the swarm scenes (0/256/1024/4096/16384 bodies, BVH and OptiX) with and without DP45. (2) Check the two close-up outlier pixels, (183, 112) and (196, 103), which differ by 46% and 14% of peak, against an independent reference. (3) Measure a kernel with DP45 alone for registers and local memory (704 → 1,128 bytes in the combined experiments kernel). Try accumulating weighted stage sums instead of keeping k1–k6 live. (4) Add a Lean correspondence/fixture for the DP45 stage arithmetic and controller rules, alongside the existing RK4 obligations. (5) Rerun the classification regressions with DP45 as the controller, then flip the default. Keep RK4 behind a flag for one release. Update README, CHANGELOG and the performance tables when the default changes.
- [ ] **P1.2 — Compile genuinely specialised render paths.** Impact high / effort medium / risk medium. Split single-Kerr scalar thermal intensity from general binary/polarized/swarm paths with compile-time specialisation where supported by cuda-oxide. `Ray` carries a screen vector and `geodesic_rhs` transports it; `sample_plasma` has an existing thermal intensity shortcut but the generic integration state is still large. Only omit screen transport if output and transfer physics provably never depend on it (e.g. appropriate scalar-only model; Stokes coupling can alter I in other models). Make one-source metric path use a fixed source instead of a general loop. Keep generic fallback. Compare 255-register baseline against measured variant counts and output; add mode-specific tests, including hot flow, warped disk and polarization. [R1, R3, R9]
- [x] **P1.3 — Investigate structured metric contractions instead of materialising matrices.** **Done 2026-11-25.** Each source's contractions are built without the three derivative matrices. The inverse stays generic, so binaries are unchanged. One- and two-hole scenes compute each source once per stage, for both the metric and the right-hand side. DP45 is 8–12% faster in `dp45_controller_benchmark`, and the close-up is about 8% faster. Images are unchanged. A GPU test checks the structured form against the matrix form and against the binary64 `certified::Kerr` right-hand side. `CudaStructuredRhs.lean` proves the exact identity. Open: a rounding bound for the structured program (the proved 3e-4 bound covers only `geodesic_rhs_matrix`). See `RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md`. Original item: Impact high / effort high / risk high. In [`add_kerr_fields`, `scene_metric_fields`, `geodesic_rhs`](https://github.com/gregorycarnegie/black_hole/blob/e9ce1b95f31602d84503066ecc552884528e2a01/tools/cuda/kernels.rs#L1247-L1409), exploit isolated Kerr–Schild rank-one form g=η+2Hll and inverse g⁻¹=η⁻¹−2H l^μ l^ν (null l in the background). Derive gradient contractions directly using H, l and their derivatives; avoid holding three 4×4 matrices if RHS only needs contractions. For binary superposition do not use the single-source inverse formula; keep current valid generic approach unless independently derived. Compare algebra with independent FP64 fixtures and update Lean correspondence. Test operation ordering, cancellations, poles and horizon. [R8]
- [ ] **P1.4 — Tune register lifetime, block shape and target architecture together.** Impact high / effort medium / risk low-medium. Ada has 65,536 32-bit registers/SM and 48 maximum warps; at ~255 registers/thread only about 8 warps can fit by register count (16.7% theoretical occupancy), subject to allocation granularity/other limits. Test shortening variable scopes, less aggressive inlining of cold code, specialised variants and supported register-cap experiments; profile spill loads/stores and eligible warps, not just occupancy. Sweep 64/128/256-thread shapes including 32×2, 32×4, 32×8 and 16×16; compare SM 8.0 shipped PTX JIT against a supported SM 8.9-specific build, preserving fallback. Record PTX build version and SASS/driver attributes; cuda-oxide flag support must be verified, not assumed. [R1–R3]
- [ ] **P1.5 — Implement a hybrid long-tail ray scheduler, not a full rewrite first.** Impact medium-high / effort high / risk medium. Start with a bounded first pass of N accepted steps (e.g. 64/128/256 as sweep values); complete ordinary rays in the existing local state and persist only unfinished rays. Compact surviving indices with device scan/select or warp/block aggregated appends (CUB is a reference, not a required Rust dependency). Resume in separate kernels, optionally group by vacuum/plasma and critical clearance. Maintain SoA or AoSoA buffers for position, p, screen, h, affine sum, coordinate time, Stokes, transmission, accumulated emission, retry/guard state, iteration and pixel ID; recompute metrics if cheaper than storage, preserve arithmetic/order where exact comparison required. Stable pixel IDs and deterministic jitter; bounded capacity, overflow fallback, no per-step host readback. Sweep chunk size and compare queue traffic/launch cost against idle-lane savings after P0.3. A full wavefront approach is a fallback experiment only if hybrid wins. [R9, R11, R12]
- [ ] **P1.6 — Selectively improve precision and stagnation handling.** Impact medium-high for accuracy / effort medium-high / risk high. Track affine/coordinate-time progress separately. Test compensated summation or split high/low accumulation; checkpoint and retry ambiguous rays in FP64 or independent high-precision reference. Promotion after FP32 drift alone cannot recover lost accuracy; preserve original conditions or a validated checkpoint. Avoid global fast math, FTZ or indiscriminate FP16/TF32; the snapshot audit has already shown subnormal coefficient failures. Precision policy needs tests for conserved quantities, screen basis and classification, and bounded runtime. **Unresolved band (gates P1.1b, decided 2026-11-24):** end a ray as unresolved, not captured or escaped, when its capture margin is within the uncertainty band. Apply the same policy to RK4 and DP45. Validate the band against the binary64 reference across step factors 0.11/0.12/0.13, counting binary32 rounding as well as integration error. Require zero wrong definitive answers. Check that rays safely away from the critical boundary still resolve, and report each controller's unresolved count so the test measures how often it can answer. **Band done 2026-11-24:** rays that have swept more than `UNRESOLVED_WINDING` = 14 rad end unresolved, in the renderer (exit `near_critical`, no sky) and in `bh_test_controller_classify`. The classification test passes with zero wrong definitive answers for RK4 and DP45 at all three factors. Rays with |margin| > 1e-4 resolve, and 147–163 of 512 rays per run stay unresolved. The close-up loses 7 of 120,000 pixels to the band. Remaining P1.6 work: compensated summation, FP64 retry, and calibrating the band for binaries. See [RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md](RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md), section "Unresolved band". [R6, R10]

### P1 — Algorithmic alternative for isolated Kerr

- [ ] **P1.7 — Implement a CPU FP64 separated Kerr reference before GPU production.** Impact high / effort high / risk high. Derive energy-normalized constants E, Lz, Q from the camera tetrad and use radial/polar potentials in a spin-aligned frame. Map Cartesian Kerr–Schild state to the analytic formulation, account for time/azimuth offsets, parameter conventions and momentum signs; handle radial/polar roots and turning points, Schwarzschild and near-extremal limits. Compare independent formulations: Gralla–Lupsasca Jacobi elliptic curves and Dexter–Agol Carlson integrals. Start with event and direction calculations; make critical or ill-conditioned cases fall back rather than misclassify. [R4, R5]
- [ ] **P1.8 — Prototype a hybrid analytic/numeric vacuum propagator.** Impact potentially high / effort high / risk high. First support isolated stationary Kerr, no swarm or intersecting geometry, a restricted camera/disk configuration and vacuum intervals proven outside transfer support. Use separated solution for escape/horizon or next disk-support boundary, not merely equatorial crossing for the existing finite-thickness disk. Reconstruct direction and any required time, momentum and screen data. Retain numerical integration and adaptive transfer quadrature inside plasma, and for unsupported scene/near-critical roots. Benchmark FP32/FP64 elliptic evaluation cost and branching versus remaining RK4 work; no claimed speedup. For a truly infinitesimal disk an optional analytic surface model could use crossings directly, but this is different physics and must be labelled, not a silent replacement. Binary, Ellis and dynamic metrics retain their existing solver. [R4, R5, R8, R13]
- [ ] **P1.9 — Decouple transfer accuracy from vacuum trajectory accuracy.** Impact medium-high / effort high / risk high. Compare dense-output trajectory interpolation plus adaptive quadrature against the current coupled stepping. Resolve sharp disk/emissivity/magnetization boundaries and verify coefficient variation; use smaller substeps where stiffness requires it and analytic constant-cell or implicit transfer only where validated. `ipole` and RAPTOR II provide method references. Do not duplicate the existing snapshot exponential or assume that its approximation applies to interactive plasma: the audit states those paths differ. First validate fixed-path transfer against independent constant-coefficient cases; then couple and check EHT snapshot separately. [R10, R14, R15]

### P2 — Sampling, geometry and OptiX-specific work

- [ ] **P2.1 — Photon-ring-aware adaptive image sampling.** Impact potentially high / effort high / risk high. Inspired by AART's nonuniform image grid, mark tiles with classification boundaries, high variation in escape direction, disk crossings, high image gradients or near-critical constants. Trace full-resolution in these regions, coarse sampling only where bounded or explicitly preview-quality; avoid interpolating across the shadow or sharp sky texture. Use temporal stability checks under orbit/jitter; report flux, ring diameter/width, Stokes errors, edge and temporal flicker. Offer exact/full-resolution mode. Existing render scale control and accumulation should be reused. [R13]
- [ ] **P2.2 — Improve the software BVH before expanding OptiX.** Impact high at very large swarm counts / effort medium-high / risk medium. Measure CPU build/upload and GPU traversal separately. Test refitting/rebuilding policy, GPU construction and slice widths, preserve swept time intervals and conservative padding. Brute force remains reference for all fallback cases. At 16,384 bodies existing host rebuild is ~75 ms; smaller scenes do not justify large investment unless measured. [R16]
- [ ] **P2.3 — Reduce OptiX queue bandwidth and synchronisation.** Impact medium / effort high / risk medium-high. [`OptixRender::render`, L531–648](https://github.com/gregorycarnegie/black_hole/blob/e9ce1b95f31602d84503066ecc552884528e2a01/src/compute/optix.rs#L531-L648) preallocates three arrays of 32/48/16 bytes per entry and synchronises per band before reading queue count, then before reuse. Test tighter belt/candidate filtering only with conservative bounds; measure per-band queue distribution, bytes and duplicate work. Explore warp/block-aggregated reservation; keep exact states or use a separately validated replay/checkpoint design to reduce memory (replay can cost more than saved bandwidth). Persistent SBT and query buffers, explicit lifetime/event ownership, async count readback or fixed-size guarded launch as experiments; no assumption that optixLaunch accepts a device-side count. Fixed-capacity launch must skip inactive indices and be benchmarked against wasted threads. Double-buffer bands only if overlap exceeds memory pressure and lifetime complexity. Preserve overflow brute-force and earliest-hit semantics. [R11, R16, R17]
- [ ] **P2.4 — Evaluate persistent motion GAS updates and resource reuse.** Impact medium at large swarm / effort medium / risk medium. Existing `SegmentPipeline::build` uses BUILD, PREFER_FAST_TRACE, and 53 motion keys. Test ALLOW_UPDATE + UPDATE with matching topology/options, persistent buffers, and periodic rebuild when quality degrades; compare build and trace times, memory usage, exact retarded-time hit semantics, dynamic padding and key changes. Cache stable SBT headers and records; measure stack size using version-matched API and explicit stack configuration if supported. Payload semantics for current three words require exact verification against SDK 9.1 and inline PTX ABI; do not replace exact any-hit test with closest padded proxy or terminate on first candidate. This extends first-pass items; not a new unimplemented claim. [R16, R17]
- [ ] **P2.5 — SER experiment only if measured OptiX shader divergence is substantial.** Impact low/uncertain in current path / effort medium-high / risk medium. Ada supports SER via OptiX. Existing raygen does a lightweight query and exact motion candidate processing occurs in any-hit. Reordering after traversal cannot retroactively improve already executed any-hit work. Consider only a supported traversal/hit-object design or explicit reorder before a genuinely expensive divergent stage; check device capability and cuda-oxide/inline PTX support. Minimise live state across reorder, benchmark against same work without SER and retain original semantics. Do not port the expensive geodesic loop into OptiX to obtain SER: prior in-loop experiment was 5–20× slower than BVH. Do not assume SER accelerates the ordinary CUDA kernel. [R17–R19]
- [ ] **P2.6 — CUDA→Bevy display interop, conditionally.** Impact low until trace accelerated / effort medium-high / risk medium. Current accumulation and readback are small compared with geodesics in recorded tests; use Systems to measure actual upload and input-to-display delay. Test pinned asynchronous readback before graphics external memory/semaphore interop (Vulkan/D3D12, actual Bevy/wgpu backend capability required). Preserve resize, device loss, accumulation reset and correct synchronisation. [R1, R20]
- [ ] **P2.7 — Avoid speculative hardware substitutions.** Do not use tensor cores for general per-ray ODEs without a validated batched formulation and precision analysis. Do not use generic AI denoising to claim physical accuracy or remove photon-ring detail. RT cores accelerate bounded geometry queries, not curved null geodesics. Consider hardware RT for mesh-heavy future scenes only, with conservative curved-path chord bounds and exact candidate refinement.

## Reproduction and acceptance checklist

- [ ] Record baseline and experiment outputs in `RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md` with commit/PTX hashes, raw CSVs and device/driver details; no tool-instrumented wall times presented as normal timing.
- [ ] Use benchmark matrix: 400×300 and 960×540, single thermal, binary, polarized close-up hot flow EVPA, warped disk, spin ±0.82/±0.99/±0.999, Schwarzschild, distant view radius 160, near-horizon/near-critical rays, patterned sky, still/orbiting, swarm 0/256/1024/4096/16384 where test permits, and separate Ellis/snapshot regressions.
- [ ] Compare baseline versus candidate under equivalent accuracy requirements. For arithmetic-preserving changes expect exact output, classifications and deterministic behavior; for numerical changes require independent references and the existing accuracy targets, plus explain all differences. Log unresolved separately; never hide them as captured. For existing failing cases require no regression and track progress toward target, without describing this as full certification.
- [ ] Verify capture/escape/event ordering, convergence, geodesic invariants, Stokes positivity/passivity, screen/redshift, high optical/Faraday depth, boundary masks, swarm exact moving-body intersections and overflow fallback. Update affected Lean obligations and fixture hashes; do not claim tests prove whole-trajectory correctness.
- [ ] Run normal CPU, GPU and verification checks; maintain original PTX fallback and runtime switches. For OptiX compare with brute force and software BVH at equal scene; only switch default if end-to-end benefit survives all supported cases.

No SDK headers/generated bindings in repository.

## Suggested implementation sequence

1. P0.1/2/3: repeat baselines, improve reference and evaluate guarded early-capture default.
2. P0.4 + P1.2/4: low-cost extrapolation, specialised intensity kernel and register pressure before major redesign.
3. P1.1/3: embedded RK and structured single-source contractions as separate experiments.
4. P1.5/6: compact remaining long-tail rays and selective precision only if post-optimisation data supports them.
5. P1.7/8/9: reference, analytic vacuum and transfer hybrid as an independent experimental backend.
6. P2: photon-ring sampling and geometry work as justified by actual target workloads; keep OptiX opt-in.

## Scope and limitations

This is a targeted literature and source audit, not an exhaustive systematic review. GitHub connector exposed a concrete commit and pinned source ranges; some repository performance-document source anchors may be stale after edits, so use symbols/current code. The prior Deep Research report summary was recovered from the library, but its standalone Markdown file was not found in the repository. The ACM Digital Library itself denied access; its paper was available through NVIDIA Research and Eurographics. GTC material was reviewed via the indexed session transcript, not full video playback. Other papers were assessed from accessible abstracts, publication pages and indexed excerpts rather than a line-by-line review of all full texts. No profiling, compilation, physics validation or benchmark runs were performed for this pass. Proposed priorities, acceptance performance threshold and kernel designs are audit recommendations, not user-specified requirements or verified performance results. No files in GitHub were modified.
